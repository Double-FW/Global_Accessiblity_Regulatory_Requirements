# Global website accessibility: regulatory overviews, outcomes and reporting

Prepared for Double FW Ltd  
Version 1.0 · Reviewed 4 November 2026

## Purpose and scope

This document expands the earlier global inventory into overviews of all 16 listed legal or governmental frameworks, followed by VPAT/Accessibility Conformance Report guidance. It covers their digital-service focus, technical baseline, intended outcomes, public information, reporting structures, and other material duties.

It summarises provisions relevant to accessibility delivery. It is not a clause-by-clause restatement of every Act, a complete worldwide inventory, or a determination that a particular organisation is in scope. National implementation, organisational size, service type, dates, exceptions and procurement terms can change the result.

**Legal duty**, **government mandate**, **advisory guidance**, and **procurement evidence** are different kinds of authority. The profiles preserve these distinctions. Practical design examples describe ways to assess an outcome; they are not automatically additional statutory requirements.

**Material update to the earlier inventory:** Canada’s adopted 2025 digital-technology amendments add phased technical, training, assessment and accessibility-statement duties. These sit alongside existing accessibility plans and progress reports. Federal US reporting also extends beyond supplier VPATs. Both are covered below.

## Reading the requirements

| Requirement type | What it establishes | Evidence it usually needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technical conformance | Whether applicable clauses or success criteria are met | Requirement-level tests, environments, results and exceptions |
| Functional outcome | Whether a person can perceive, understand and operate a function using the relevant access mode | Task and interaction evidence, including AT and human evaluation where needed |
| Equality or accommodation | Whether discriminatory disadvantage is prevented or reasonably addressed | Barrier, circumstances, effective adjustment and actual access |
| Public transparency | What users must be told about accessibility and obtaining help | Accurate statement, service information, contact and escalation routes |
| Organisational process | How barriers are prevented and corrected over time | Policies, plans, training, procurement, ownership, consultation and change control |
| Regulatory reporting | Information submitted to or published for an authority | Jurisdiction-specific forms, headings, assessment returns and deadlines |
| Procurement reporting | How a supplier describes a particular product’s support | Version-specific ACR, testing methods, limitations and substantiated explanations |

A technical pass, a public statement, an organisational policy and a satisfactory user experience answer different questions. None should be used to conceal missing evidence in another category. Satisfaction, fixed research sample sizes and statistical significance are not universal legal pass conditions in the instruments below.

## 1. EU Web Accessibility Directive

**Authority and scope.** [Directive (EU) 2016/2102](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2016/2102/oj), implemented through national law, covers qualifying public-sector websites and mobile apps.

**Focus and technical approach.** Article 4 requires perceivable, operable, understandable and robust services. The relevant provisions in Annex A of EN 301 549 support the presumption of conformity. The Commission identifies V3.2.1, based on WCAG 2.1, as the harmonised WAD reference. Additional applicable provisions cover matters beyond WCAG. Publishing a newer standard does not itself change the legally cited version.

**User outcomes.** People should be able to obtain information and use public services, including documents, forms and transactions, with relevant assistive technology.

**Public information and reporting.** Article 7 requires a regularly updated accessibility statement, inaccessible-content explanations, alternatives where appropriate, feedback and enforcement routes. [Decision 2018/1523](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32018D1523) supplies the model. [Decision 2018/1524](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32018D1524) sets monitoring and reporting methods. Member States report every three years; this is not a reporting quota imposed on each website.

**Other duties and limits.** Assess exclusions and Article 5 burden claims separately. National law may be stronger. Training, awareness, stakeholder cooperation and effective enforcement also feature in the Directive.

Sources: [WAD provisions](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A32016L2102), [Commission harmonisation guidance](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/web-accessibility-directive-standards-and-harmonisation), and the two decisions above.

## 2. EU European Accessibility Act

**Authority and scope.** [Directive (EU) 2019/882](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj), implemented nationally, concerns specified consumer products and services. Covered services include e-commerce, consumer banking, e-books, electronic communications, access to audiovisual media services and specified passenger-transport elements. It is not a general law for every business website.

**Primary focus.** Accessible design and operation of the complete covered product/service, supported by information, interfaces and service processes. WCAG evidence is relevant to web interfaces but does not settle every EAA requirement. Presumption-of-conformity claims must identify the relevant Official Journal citation and its coverage.

### Annex I: the seven sections

| Section | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| I | Product information, instructions, interface/functionality and support |
| II | Packaging and instructions outside the product, excluding self-service terminals |
| III | General service provision, information, websites/apps and support |
| IV | Additional requirements for individual service categories |
| V | Accessible answering of emergency communications to 112 |
| VI | Relevant features/functions under the Article 24(2) cross-legislation conformity route |
| VII | Functional performance criteria and their conditions of use |

Annex II gives non-binding examples; it is not another set of mandatory design techniques. Annex III addresses built environments where national law adopts that option.

### Functional performance criteria: Annex I, Section VII

These criteria address otherwise uncovered functions and tightly conditioned alternative technical solutions. They do not permit an operator to disregard applicable requirements simply because one task succeeds.

| Criterion | Required direction of the outcome |
| --- | --- |
| (a) Without vision | Visual operation has a non-visual mode. |
| (b) Limited vision | A mode supports operation with limited vision. |
| (c) Without colour perception | A visual mode works without distinguishing colours. |
| (d) Without hearing | Auditory operation has a mode not requiring hearing. |
| (e) Limited hearing | Enhanced audio supports operation with limited hearing. |
| (f) Without vocal capability | Required vocal input has a non-vocal route. |
| (g) Limited manipulation or strength | Alternatives avoid fine manipulation, strength or simultaneous controls. |
| (h) Limited reach | Operational elements and manual modes accommodate limited reach/strength. |
| (i) Photosensitive seizures | Product visual modes avoid seizure-triggering operation. |
| (j) Limited cognition | A mode includes features simplifying operation. |
| (k) Privacy | Accessibility-feature use has a privacy-preserving mode. |

Practical interpretation: assess the task across relevant modes and combinations of need. A forced telephone route may exclude a person without speech; an accessible payment route should also preserve privacy. These examples are assessment questions, not newly prescribed technologies.

### Service-specific objectives

Section IV adds requirements beyond generic website operation:

- Communications: real-time text and, where relevant, synchronised total conversation.
- Audiovisual-service access: accessible discovery, selection and control of access features.
- Transport: applicable travel information, electronic ticketing and related digital interfaces.
- Banking: accessible identification, signatures/security/payment functions and understandable information not exceeding CEFR B2 complexity.
- E-books: usable navigation, structure and AT compatibility; protection measures must not block accessibility.
- E-commerce: available accessibility information and accessible identification, security, signatures and payment.

The B2 provision concerns consumer banking; it is not a universal reading-level rule for all services.

### Public information, schemas and accountability

Article 13/Annex V concern accessible service descriptions, operation and explanations of how requirements are met, normally in terms/conditions or an equivalent document, plus continuing conformity arrangements.

The service information must be available accessibly in written and oral form while the service operates. Providers must correct non-conformity, inform competent national authorities as required by Article 13 and cooperate with them. This is an operational accountability duty, not simply publication of a commitment.

Annex IV addresses product technical documentation and conformity assessment. Product EU declarations and CE marking are distinct from website/service statements. Annex VI supports burden assessment. Microenterprise service exemptions, content exclusions, transitions and Article 14 exceptions require separate scope decisions.

A VPAT can support evidence but is not a prescribed EAA service-information schema.

Most core requirements apply from 28 June 2025, subject to specific transitions. Urban/suburban/regional transport has narrower coverage. Article 14 burden assessments must be reasoned; service providers renew a disproportionate-burden assessment after relevant changes or authority requests and at least every five years. That cycle must not be imported into the WAD. Enforcement routes and penalties are implemented nationally.

Sources: [Directive and annexes](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj), [official published text hosted by Luxembourg](https://accessibilite-produits-services.public.lu/dam-assets/lgislation/celex-32019l0882-en-txt-1.pdf), [EU summary](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/EN/legal-content/summary/accessibility-of-products-and-services.html), [Commission EAA overview](https://commission.europa.eu/strategy-and-policy/policies/justice-and-fundamental-rights/disability/european-accessibility-act-eaa_en). Sector and technical explanations should be checked against national implementing provisions.

## 3. UK Public Sector Bodies Accessibility Regulations

**Authority and scope.** The [Public Sector Bodies (Websites and Mobile Applications) (No. 2) Accessibility Regulations 2018](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/952) apply to covered UK public-sector websites/apps, with specified exemptions and older-content conditions.

**Focus and technical approach.** Accessible digital public services plus transparency. Current government guidance identifies WCAG 2.2 AA as the expected technical route. Intranets/extranets have specific coverage rules; mobile-app scope differs from staff-only web systems.

**User outcomes and design objectives.** Access information, navigate, complete forms, identify errors and obtain a usable result. Assess actual service journeys rather than only a homepage or automated score.

**Statements and reporting.** Publish an accessible statement, keep it under review and use the [government model](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/model-accessibility-statement). It identifies scope, compliance status, inaccessible content and reasons, alternatives, preparation/assessment information, feedback and enforcement. The model recommends at least annual accuracy review. Government monitoring is separate from an organisation’s own assessment.

**Other duties.** Burden claims require justification; exclusions do not mean technical success. Equality legislation continues to apply.

Sources: [Scope and requirements](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/accessibility-requirements-for-public-sector-websites-and-apps), [assessment/publication guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/make-your-website-or-app-accessible-and-publish-an-accessibility-statement), regulations and model above.

## 4. Great Britain Equality Act

**Authority and scope.** The [Equality Act 2010](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15) concerns discrimination across services, public functions, employment and other fields. Northern Ireland has separate disability-discrimination legislation.

**Focus and technical approach.** Equality of access and reasonable adjustments, rather than a prescribed website checklist. Sections 20/29 and Schedule 2 are central to services. The services duty is anticipatory: consider disabled people generally rather than waiting for each person to encounter a barrier. The Act does not prescribe a universal WCAG version.

**User outcomes and design objectives.** Remove substantial disadvantage through reasonable changes to practices, auxiliary aids or service provision. A technical target should support effective access; satisfying it does not automatically answer every discrimination question.

**Public policy and reporting.** No general website-specific statement or VPAT requirement arises solely from the Act. Section 149 adds the Public Sector Equality Duty. Specific-duty regulations can require public equality information and objectives; arrangements differ across England, Scotland and Wales.

**Other provisions.** The Act covers multiple protected characteristics and forms of discrimination. An accessibility policy is evidence of intent, not proof that adjustments work.

Sources: [Services reasonable-adjustment explanation](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/notes/division/3/16/19), [Schedule 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/schedule/2), [government equality/PSED guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/equality-act-2010-guidance).

## 5. USA Section 508

**Authority and scope.** [Section 508 and the Revised Standards](https://www.access-board.gov/ict/) concern ICT federal agencies develop, procure, maintain or use, including websites, software, documents and support.

**Focus and technical approach.** WCAG 2.0 A/AA applies within the Standards’ scope. Additional ICT provisions cover software interoperability, hardware, documentation and support. Comparable access for federal employees and the public is central.

**Functional outcomes.** Chapter 3 covers operation without vision, with limited vision, without colour perception, without/with limited hearing, without speech, with limited manipulation, with limited reach/strength and with limited language/cognitive/learning abilities. These criteria apply under the Standards’ scoping and equivalent-facilitation rules, not as an unrestricted replacement for technical clauses.

**Reporting and records.** Supplier ACRs support procurement. Agencies also participate in annual governmentwide Section 508 assessment reporting and the DOJ biennial process. Those agency returns differ from product ACRs.

**Other provisions.** User-needs identification, written exception/best-meets determinations and alternative access are material controls.

Sources: [Standards, E101–E208 and Chapter 3](https://www.access-board.gov/ict/), [agency reporting requirements](https://www.section508.gov/manage/agency-508-reporting-requirements/), [ACR guidance](https://www.section508.gov/sell/acr-vpat-faq/).

## 6. USA ADA Title II

**Authority and scope.** [ADA Title II regulations](https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/regulations/title-ii-2010-regulations/) cover state/local government programmes and services. Subpart H establishes specific web/app requirements, including contracted delivery.

**Focus and technical approach.** WCAG 2.1 A/AA success criteria and conformance requirements. Following the 2026 extension, technical compliance dates are 26 April 2027 for entities with populations of at least 50,000, and 26 April 2028 for smaller entities and special districts.

**User outcomes.** People must be able to access government information and services. Outsourcing does not transfer the public entity’s accountability.

**Public information and reporting.** The web rule does not prescribe a universal website statement or VPAT filing. Existing Title II duties include ADA information, and, for entities meeting the applicable employee threshold, a responsible coordinator and grievance procedure. Evidence of exceptions and equivalent facilitation is separate from a vendor report.

**Other provisions.** Archived content, certain older documents, third-party content and other defined categories have specific exceptions. Fundamental-alteration/undue-burden and minimal-impact provisions have conditions. General ADA duties remain relevant before and after the technical deadlines.

Sources: [DOJ rule fact sheet and 2026 update](https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/), [regulations](https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/regulations/title-ii-2010-regulations/).

## 7. USA ADA Title III

**Authority and scope.** [Title III regulations](https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/regulations/title-iii-regulations/) concern covered private public accommodations. The treatment of online-only businesses and required connection to physical premises varies across courts.

**Focus and technical approach.** Full and equal enjoyment, non-discrimination, reasonable modifications and effective communication. DOJ has not adopted a general Title III technical web rule equivalent to Title II’s WCAG 2.1 rule. WCAG provides useful evidence, but a contract or court order can impose a particular baseline.

**User outcomes and design objectives.** Disabled customers should be able to obtain the business’s goods/services and receive effective information. Assess purchasing, booking, identification, payment, support and recovery where they form part of the service.

**Statements and reporting.** No universal federal Title III website statement or VPAT submission is prescribed. A settlement, court order, procurement agreement or sector-specific law can require audits, training, policies, reporting and named owners.

**Other provisions.** Relevant defences and limits, including fundamental alteration and undue burden for auxiliary aids/services, require their own legal analysis. Do not import Title II deadlines or all its web exceptions into Title III.

Sources: [DOJ web guidance](https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/), [federal explanation of Title II/III distinction](https://www.section508.gov/blog/do-section-508-accessibility-standards-apply-to-mywebsite/), regulations above.

## 8. Canada Accessible Canada framework

**Authority and scope.** The [Accessible Canada Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/A-0.6/) and [Accessible Canada Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2021-241/FullText.html) concern federally regulated entities. Transport and broadcasting/telecommunications have additional regulator-specific arrangements.

### Existing planning, feedback and reporting

The framework addresses barriers across ICT, employment, communications, procurement, programmes/services and other areas. It requires applicable entities to consult disabled people, publish plans and describe feedback processes.

Plans operate on a three-year cycle; progress reports normally occupy the intervening years. Required headings include General, applicable priority areas and Consultations; progress reports also address Feedback. Published materials must be accessible, with alternative formats and regulator notification as applicable.

### Adopted digital-technology amendments

The final [SOR/2025-255 amendments](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-12-17/html/sor-dors255-eng.html) add phased duties. Their future commencement must not be confused with the existing planning/reporting duties.

They reference CAN/ASC–EN 301 549:2024: applicable generic requirements plus web, document or software clauses. This extends beyond WCAG-only assessment.

| General category | Principal phased dates |
| --- | --- |
| Federal public-sector entities, excluding special transport/broadcasting arrangements | New/updated covered web pages: 5 December 2027. Covered documents and mobile-app obligations: 5 December 2028. |
| Large federally regulated private entities outside those special sectors | Covered new/updated web pages, documents and app obligations generally begin 5 December 2028. |
| Relevant digital staff | Training generally starts by 5 December 2027, with refresher requirements. |
| Other sizes and transport/broadcasting entities | Different scope and dates; use the regulator/entity-specific schedule. |

The amendments also introduce annual accessibility statements, procurement conformity assessments, non-conformity information and alternative-measure requirements under their conditions. Statements describe features, gaps, alternatives and improvement plans. They are separate from the organisation-wide accessibility plan.

The amendment specifies retention of relevant statements, conformity-assessment/gap-analysis reports and training records. Apply its particular four-year retention rules to those records; do not substitute them for the separate planning/reporting retention requirements.

**User outcomes.** Accessible employee/customer tasks, AT compatibility and workable alternatives when the regulatory conditions permit them.

Sources: [Current planning regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2021-241/FullText.html), [progress-report requirements](https://www.chrc-ccdp.gc.ca/organizations/accessible-canada-act-responsibilities/accessibility-progress-reports), [final amendment text](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-12-17/html/sor-dors255-eng.html), [entity-specific implementation guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/programs/accessible-canada-regulations-guidance/digital-technologies-phase-1.html).

## 9. Ontario AODA

**Authority and scope.** The [Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/05a11) and [Integrated Accessibility Standards Regulation 191/11](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/110191) combine technical access with organisational processes.

**Technical focus.** Section 14 generally requires designated public-sector organisations and businesses/non-profits with at least 50 employees to make covered public web content conform to WCAG 2.0 AA. Live captions and prerecorded audio description are excluded for these categories. Ontario government/Legislative Assembly requirements differ. Publication dates, control and practicability conditions matter.

**Policies and design objectives.** Relevant entities need accessibility policies, a public commitment and multi-year plan, reviewed at least every five years. The broader framework includes training, accessible feedback, alternative formats, communications, employment and customer service. Dignity, independence, integration and equal opportunity guide policies and service provision.

**Reporting schema.** The provincial accessibility compliance report is an official declaration/form, not a WCAG audit or VPAT. Reporting generally runs every two years for designated public bodies and every three years for businesses/non-profits with 20+ employees. Public bodies also publish annual plan-progress status reports.

Sources: [Website scope/exceptions](https://www.ontario.ca/page/how-make-websites-accessible), [policies and plans](https://www.ontario.ca/page/how-create-accessibility-plan-and-policy), [public-body reporting](https://www.ontario.ca/page/accessibility-rules-public-sector-organizations), [business reporting](https://www.ontario.ca/page/accessibility-rules-businesses-and-non-profits).

## 10. France Article 47 and RGAA

**Authority and scope.** [Article 47 of Law 2005-102](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000037388867) and [Decree 2019-768](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/LEGIARTI000038956850/) cover public bodies, specified public-interest entities and qualifying companies at the €250 million French-turnover threshold. Separate EAA-derived obligations also apply to relevant services.

**Technical focus.** The applicable reference standard and national RGAA assessment method support technical evaluation. RGAA 4.1.2 remains the published method at review; RGAA 5 is being prepared. An RGAA audit is a test methodology, not a substitute for the underlying law.

**Outcomes and public policy.** Accessible information and service operation are supported by an accessibility lead, organisational strategy, resources and delivery arrangements.

**Statements and reporting.** The national declaration format records compliance status, audit result, barriers, exceptions, assessment date, technologies, tools, tested sample, contact and remedies. French guidance uses a 50% threshold for partial compliance; do not export it to other jurisdictions. A published multi-year scheme of up to three years and annual action plans explain implementation. Current guidance also specifies complaint handling and declaration-update triggers.

Sources: [Covered entities](https://accessibilite.numerique.gouv.fr/obligations/champ-application/), [declaration format and response requirements](https://accessibilite.numerique.gouv.fr/obligations/declaration-accessibilite/), [multi-year scheme](https://accessibilite.numerique.gouv.fr/obligations/schema-pluriannuel/), [RGAA revision status](https://www.numerique.gouv.fr/actualites/nouvelle-version-rgaa-2026/).

## 11. Australia Disability Discrimination Act

**Authority and scope.** The [Disability Discrimination Act 1992](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A04426/latest) prohibits disability discrimination across goods/services, employment and other fields, including relevant digital access.

**Technical focus.** The Act does not prescribe one universal WCAG version. The Australian Human Rights Commission’s 2025 guidelines recommend WCAG 2.2 AA and identify relevant ICT standards. These guidelines are advisory; their publication does not turn every recommended technique into legislation.

**User outcomes and design objectives.** Equal access, reasonable adjustments and avoiding discriminatory barriers. The Commission recommends embedding access through design, development, procurement, risk management and organisational processes. Its strategic guidance discusses AS EN 301 549 and AS ISO/IEC 30071.1 alongside other governance standards.

**Policies and reporting.** No general website-statement or VPAT submission requirement arises solely from the Act. Disability Action Plans provide an optional statutory planning mechanism; their use is not a universal annual website-report duty. Complaints and settlements can create specific obligations.

**Other provisions.** Relevant unjustifiable-hardship considerations require assessment. A public commitment or standard-conformance claim does not automatically resolve actual discrimination.

Sources: [Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A04426/latest), [2025 guidelines and their legal status](https://humanrights.gov.au/?a=68431), [technical guidance](https://humanrights.gov.au/?a=68429), [strategic implementation guidance](https://humanrights.gov.au/resource-hub/by-resource-type/publications/guidelines/articles/disability-rights/accessibility/chapter-2-how-provide-equal-access-digital-goods-and-services).

## 12. New Zealand Government Web Accessibility Standard

**Authority and scope.** [Web Accessibility Standard 1.2](https://www.digital.govt.nz/standards-and-guidance/nz-government-web-standards/web-accessibility-standard-1-2) is a Cabinet-mandated standard for specified government organisations, not a blanket commercial website statute.

**Technical focus.** It applies WCAG 2.2 AA to covered public/internal web pages from 17 March 2025, with explicit modifications. These address complex maps, media alternatives, live captions, audio description and inactive pages. High-stakes information/services receive particular treatment. Do not summarise it as unmodified WCAG 2.2 AA.

**Outcomes.** People should be able to obtain government information and use services through accessible pages and alternatives.

**Reporting.** When notified by the Government Chief Digital Officer, mandated organisations assess conformity and submit a report, risk assessment and risk-management plan. This is an authority-requested assurance process, not a universal VPAT filing or annual public statement scheme.

The GCDO supplies the methodology for that assessment/reporting exercise.

**Change status.** A broader Digital Accessibility Standard is under development, with publication expected in early 2027. Its draft must not be presented as a current mandatory standard.

Sources: [Current standard, including sections 3–4](https://www.digital.govt.nz/standards-and-guidance/nz-government-web-standards/web-accessibility-standard-1-2), [DAS development status](https://www.digital.govt.nz/standards-and-guidance/nz-government-web-standards/digital-accessibility-standard).

## 13. Israel Service Accessibility Regulations

**Authority and scope.** The [Service Accessibility Regulations’ internet provisions](https://www.gov.il/en/pages/internet_accessibility_regulations) sit within Israel’s disability-equality framework. Covered public services include relevant commercially provided services; “public service” does not mean government only.

**Technical focus.** Israeli Standard 5568, the required conformance level and national modifications determine web requirements. Do not replace these with an assumption of unmodified WCAG 2.2. Rules distinguish web content, documents, media, apps and certain third-party content.

**User outcomes.** People should be able to access service information and functionality with relevant assistive technologies and alternatives.

**Public information.** The required accessibility declaration and information on arrangements explain accessibility and contact routes. Commission guidance recommends bringing relevant digital and organisational arrangements together, including accessibility-coordinator information where applicable. These arrangements can extend beyond the website.

**Exceptions and evidence.** Turnover, technical limitations and undue-burden provisions have conditions. Some require a formal request and supporting records. An operator should not invent an exemption from a poor audit result. No universal VPAT-based statutory report is prescribed.

Sources: [Regulatory provisions](https://www.gov.il/en/pages/internet_accessibility_regulations), [Commission website/app guidance](https://www.gov.il/en/pages/website_accessibility?chapterIndex=5), [declaration guidance](https://www.gov.il/he/pages/declaration_website_accessibility?chapterIndex=3), [burden-exemption application](https://www.gov.il/he/service/application_for_exemption_internet_people_with_disabilities).

## 14. South Korea Disability Discrimination Act

**Authority and scope.** [Disability Discrimination Act Article 21](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1031811973) and [Enforcement Decree Article 14](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1030492419) concern equal access to electronic information and communication by covered actors.

**Technical focus.** The Decree expressly addresses accessible websites. The national standard is Korean Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, KWCAG. **KWCAG 2.2 is not the same document/version as W3C WCAG 2.2.** National criteria and procurement conditions must be identified rather than inferring equivalence from the number.

**User outcomes and design objectives.** Equivalent information access and website-service use regardless of physical or technical circumstances. The framework also covers communication supports beyond website code.

**Reporting and assurance.** The web-accessibility quality certification scheme includes documentary, expert and user review. It must not be described as a universal annual reporting duty imposed by Article 21. Individual authorities can specify evidence: Seoul’s published guidance, for example, accepts a self-check report or relevant certification for its website launch review.

**Public policies.** Check the organisation’s actual legal, sector and procurement conditions before requiring a particular statement or certificate.

Sources: [Law](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1031811973), [current Decree provision](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1030492419), [national standard](https://www.rra.go.kr/ko/reference/kcsList_view.do?nb_seq=5247&nb_type=6), [Seoul assurance guidance](https://news.seoul.go.kr/gov/archives/47485), [certification explanation](https://www.culture.go.kr/portal/main/contents.do?menuNo=200180).

## 15. Brazil Inclusion Law

**Authority and scope.** [Law 13.146/2015](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2015/lei/l13146.htm), the Brazilian Inclusion Law, is a broad disability-rights statute. Article 63 expressly covers government websites and companies headquartered or commercially represented in Brazil.

**Technical focus.** Article 63 refers to internationally adopted accessibility practices/guidelines without naming one WCAG version. The federal eMAG framework supplies government-specific implementation guidance; it is not automatically the technical mandate for every private website.

**User outcomes and design objectives.** Access to information and communication, participation and autonomy, rather than a visual “accessible” label alone.

**Public information.** Article 63 requires a prominent accessibility symbol. This is not equivalent to proving conformance, nor a requirement to install an accessibility overlay.

**Reporting schema.** Article 120 and [Interministerial Ordinance 1/2017](https://bibliotecadigital.mj.gov.br/bitstream/1/5123/2/PRI_GM_2017_1.html) underpin federal-government detailed reporting: digital-accessibility form, non-conformity findings and work plan. The ordinance connects follow-up to management reporting. Its original deadlines are historical; they are not a new annual private-company filing deadline.

**Other provisions.** The Act also concerns education, employment, transport and other rights. Federal reporting should not be confused with a universal VPAT obligation.

Sources: [Act, especially Articles 63 and 120](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2015/lei/l13146.htm), [government legislation index for eMAG/reporting](https://www.gov.br/governodigital/pt-br/legislacao/acessibilidade), ordinance above.

## 16. South Africa equality framework

**Authority and scope.** The [Promotion of Equality and Prevention of Unfair Discrimination Act 4 of 2000](https://www.gov.za/ve/documents/promotion-equality-and-prevention-unfair-discrimination-act) is a broad equality law. It is not a dedicated WCAG website regulation.

**Focus.** Section 9 addresses disability discrimination, including obstacles to equal opportunities and failures to reasonably accommodate. Its relevance to a digital barrier follows the equality/access context; do not claim that the Act itself prescribes a website checklist.

**Technical guidance and design objectives.** Government Communication and Information System website guidance recommends at least WCAG 2.2 Level A. Separate content guidance calls for headings, descriptive links, accessible documents, contrast, keyboard access and AT compatibility.

**Policies, evidence and reporting.** Government guidance requires checking before publication and continuing monitoring. Neither that guidance nor the general Act should be represented as a universal commercial VPAT-filing scheme. Applicable departmental, contractual and sector requirements need separate identification.

**User outcomes.** Remove barriers that unfairly restrict access and provide reasonable accommodation. Broader usability practices in government guidance are useful delivery objectives; their advisory status should remain visible.

Sources: [Act](https://www.gov.za/ve/documents/promotion-equality-and-prevention-unfair-discrimination-act), [consolidated Section 9](https://lawlibrary.org.za/akn/za/act/2000/4/eng%402024-04-03), [GCIS accessibility guidance](https://www.gcis.gov.za/guidelines/website/website-accessibility), [content maintenance guidance](https://www.gcis.gov.za/guidelines/website/maintain-content).

## 17. VPAT and ACR reporting

**Status.** The Information Technology Industry Council’s VPAT is a template. A completed report is an Accessibility Conformance Report. It records a particular product/service’s support for selected criteria; it is not a regulation, certification or substitute for statutory reporting.

| Edition | Standards covered |
| --- | --- |
| 508 | Revised US Section 508 |
| EU | EN 301 549 |
| WCAG | WCAG |
| INT | Combined coverage of the above |

Choose the edition and standards versions matching the actual contract and jurisdiction.

**Report structure.** Identify product/version, date, scope, contact, evaluation methods and applicable standards. Criterion tables record Supports, Partially Supports, Does Not Support or Not Applicable, with explanatory remarks. Those categories are reporting terms, not a legal pass mark.

**Evidence and limits.** Claims should reflect actual testing of the specified configuration. Review the explanations, unsupported features, platform coverage and exceptions. A report completed by the supplier is not automatically independently verified. Conformance reporting also does not establish satisfaction or every real-world task outcome.

Sources: [ITI template editions and instructions](https://www.itic.org/policy/accessibility/vpat), [US ACR/VPAT FAQ](https://www.section508.gov/sell/acr-vpat-faq/), [ACR preparation guidance](https://www.section508.gov/sell/how-to-create-acr-with-vpat/).

This table compares the purposes of the formats described above. It does not create additional duties.

| Format | Principal audience | Typical content | What it does not establish alone |

| EAA service information | Consumers and competent authorities | Service description, operation, applicable requirements and continuing conformity | Product CE conformity or all national duties |
| Product technical file/declaration | Authorities and supply chain | Product identity, design/conformity evidence and applicable exceptions | Website-service transparency |

| Ontario compliance report | Provincial regulator | Official responses/declarations about applicable obligations | Criterion-level audit results |

None of the statutory formats above should be replaced with the optional common schema below without retaining the jurisdiction’s required wording, headings, publication route and submission process.

### Practical interpretation of user outcomes

For a public or commercial web task, record what a person must accomplish, the access mode used, the information and decisions needed, errors/recovery and whether help is unexpectedly required. Test technical criteria and assess that experience separately.

For example, in an online purchase:

- Semantic names and keyboard access supply technical evidence.
- Completing identification, payment and confirmation supplies task evidence.
- Accessible terms and required service information supply transparency evidence.
- A support route supplies process evidence.
- An ACR supplies structured supplier assertions, which need evaluation.

These are recommended evidence distinctions. They must not be relabelled as universal statutory research metrics or used to weaken an applicable technical requirement.

## Maintenance and legal boundaries

Recheck the legal text, applicable national implementation and cited technical standard before making a live conformity claim. In particular:

- Publication of a revised standard does not automatically amend a legal citation.
- A future adopted duty differs from a proposal or a current obligation.
- National provisions can add requirements to regional frameworks.
- Exclusion, burden, technical failure and untested scope are different statuses.
- A supplier contract can require stronger evidence than legislation, but cannot remove the customer’s legal duties.
- A public policy or statement must describe actual conditions; publishing it does not itself remove barriers.

This document retains the earlier inventory’s scope while expanding its substantive and reporting coverage. Its linked sources are intended to support further clause-level requirement registers, test matrices and jurisdiction-specific reporting.
